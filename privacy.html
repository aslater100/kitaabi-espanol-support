Kitaabi Español Privacy Policy

Effective November 22, 2026

Kitaabi Español (on the App Store as "Kitaabi: Spanish Learning") is an app for learning Spanish. The app has no accounts, no ads and no tracking. The developer receives nothing from the app except:

  • your name and points on the leaderboard;
  • anonymous star ratings you choose to give;
  • the price survey and usage reports from beta versions;
  • anything you send yourself.

Each is described below. This policy covers Kitaabi Español only. Kitaabi, the Arabic app, has its own policy.

What stays on your device

  • Your progress. Lesson and flashcard history, drill results, streaks, settings, and any word lists or decks you import are stored on your device.
  • Your recordings. When you practice speaking, your voice is recorded on your device so you can play it back. Kitaabi Español doesn't upload your recordings anywhere, and deletes them when they're no longer needed.
  • Your Claude API key, if you add one, is kept in your device's Keychain.

iCloud sync

If you're signed in to iCloud, Kitaabi Español syncs your progress between your iPhone, iPad and Mac. It uses your own private iCloud database, which Apple stores for you, in Kitaabi Español's own iCloud container, separate from the Arabic app's. The developer can't see or access it. You can turn iCloud off for Kitaabi Español in your device's iCloud settings.

Speech recognition (Apple)

Speaking drills turn your spoken Spanish into text using Apple's speech recognition. It runs on your device when Spanish recognition is available there. Otherwise your audio is sent to Apple to be transcribed, under Apple's privacy policy. Kitaabi Español doesn't link it to your identity, and the developer never receives it. You can refuse microphone or speech recognition access, and the rest of the app still works.

Optional Claude features (Anthropic)

Some features, such as new practice passages and example sentences, use Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic. They only work if you enter your own Anthropic API key. When you use one, the app sends the text that request needs directly from your device to Anthropic. That's the drill prompt and the transcript of your spoken answer, for example, or the word and meaning you want an example sentence for. Your audio and your progress aren't sent. Anthropic handles this data under its own terms and privacy policy, and links it to your API account, not to the developer. If you don't add a key, nothing is ever sent to Anthropic.

Crash reports (Apple)

If you've chosen to share analytics with app developers in your device's settings, Apple sends the developer crash and performance reports, such as crash logs and launch times. Apple collects these, not Kitaabi Español, and they don't identify you. You can turn this off under Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements.

Anonymous ratings

Kitaabi Español asks for a quick star rating after a listening or reading passage, after each Sentence Builder sentence, and after each flashcard. You can skip it.

If you rate, the app sends the developer the stars, which passage, sentence or card you rated, its skill and level, the app's build number, and the date. Under five stars you can also write what the issue was. That comment goes with the rating, so leave personal details out of it. A rating doesn't include your name, your device, your answers or your recordings.

Ratings go to the app's public CloudKit database, which Apple runs. Only the developer can read them: other people using the app can't see your ratings, and the app itself only sends them.

Like everything saved to a CloudKit public database, a rating carries an anonymous ID that Apple assigns. It isn't your Apple Account, and the developer can't find out who it belongs to. It is the same for all your ratings, though, so they can be told apart from other people's. The developer uses ratings only to find lessons, passages, drills and cards that need work.

Leaderboard

Kitaabi Español has a weekly and an all-time leaderboard. If you're signed in to iCloud, you're on it automatically. It shows the first name you chose when you set up the app, and your points for the week and for all time. Everyone using Kitaabi Español can see them. Points come from the lessons, drills and flashcards you finish. Nothing else about you or your answers is shared.

Your name and points are saved to the app's public CloudKit database, which Apple runs, with the same kind of anonymous ID described under Anonymous ratings. It isn't your Apple Account, and no sign-in is needed. Names are checked for profanity before they're shown. Anyone can report a name, and the developer may remove names that don't belong.

You can leave the leaderboard at any time under Settings → Leaderboard. Your name and points are then taken off it, and you can rejoin later. Without an iCloud account you aren't on the leaderboard.

Beta versions only: price survey

Beta versions are the ones installed from TestFlight, with a version number below 1.0. They offer a short, optional price survey on the Today screen. It asks:

  • about how much time you've spent with Kitaabi Español;
  • which part you use most;
  • what you'd pay;
  • which kind of plan you'd prefer;
  • whether an employer or school would pay for it;
  • anything else you'd like to say.

If you send it, the app sends your answers with the build number and the date, the same way as ratings and with the same anonymous ID. The developer uses the answers only to set the app's price. The App Store version (1.0 and later) doesn't have the survey.

Beta versions only: usage reports

Beta versions also send the developer a usage report on their own, at most once an hour. It covers:

  • which screens you opened;
  • drills done, and how often features are used;
  • the types of files you imported, not their names;
  • errors and likely crashes, and the crash and hang diagnostics the system gives the app;
  • your device model, the app version, and the date you started using the app.

It goes to the same CloudKit database with the same anonymous ID, and only the developer can read it. It never includes your answers, recordings, transcripts, or anything you type into a bug report. The App Store version doesn't send it.

Feedback and bug reports

If you send a bug report from Settings, it opens in your own email app so you can read it before sending. It may include a usage report: which screens you used and for how long, plus your device model and app version. It doesn't include your recordings or your API key. The developer receives it only if you press Send, and uses it only to fix bugs and improve the app.

Children

Kitaabi Español isn't directed at children under 13 and doesn't knowingly collect personal information from anyone.

Deleting your data

Deleting the app removes everything stored on that device. To take your name and points off the leaderboard, leave it under Settings → Leaderboard before deleting the app.

To clear your synced progress too, use Reset progress in Kitaabi Español's Settings while iCloud is on, which clears it on all your devices. You can also delete Kitaabi Español's data in your iCloud settings. To have a bug report you've emailed deleted, write to the address below.
